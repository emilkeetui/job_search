# Networking for Economic Consulting Jobs

Context: Cornell applied econ PhD, JMP on coal mining × drinking water quality.
Target firms: Analysis Group, Cornerstone, Brattle, CRA, Compass Lexecon, NERA,
Edgeworth, Bates White. Going on the market fall 2026.

---

## The core mechanic: informational interviews

You're not asking for a job — you're asking for a 20–30 min conversation about
their work. Low-cost, flattering, and an accepted norm in consulting (associates
are often expected to do recruiting outreach).

The funnel:
**reach out → 20-min coffee chat → they remember you in the fall → referral.**
A referral moves your application from the pile to the "interview this person"
stack. That's the entire game.

---

## Who to contact (priority order)

1. **Cornell econ PhD alumni at these firms** — highest hit rate (shared advisor,
   department, job-market experience). Warmest channel.
2. **Cornell alumni broadly** (undergrad, masters, anyone) at the target firms.
3. **Your advisor's / committee's former students** — ask your advisor directly:
   "Do you have former students in econ consulting I could talk to?" Faculty
   referrals carry weight.
4. **Second-degree connections** — labmates, friends a year or two ahead, anyone
   who can do a warm intro.

## How to find them

- **LinkedIn:** filter by company + school ("Cornell") + "Economics." Look for
  PhD Economist / Associate / Manager titles.
- **Firm websites:** team/bio pages list staff and often their PhD institution.
- **Department placement records / alumni list** — ask the DGS or placement
  coordinator who went into consulting.
- **EconJobMarket / department Slack / cohort grapevine.**

## Outreach email template

> Subject: Cornell econ PhD — quick question about Analysis Group
>
> Hi [Name],
> I'm a Cornell applied econ PhD student going on the market this fall, and I'm
> strongly considering economic consulting. I saw you did your PhD at
> [Cornell / similar program] before joining [firm] — I'd love to hear about your
> experience there. Would you have 20 minutes for a call in the next few weeks?
> Happy to work around your schedule.
> Thanks so much,
> [Name] — [one line: "JMP on the effect of coal mining on drinking water quality"]

Why it works: shared tie + small clear ask + signals serious candidate + plants
a hook with the one-line JMP.

---

## Running the call (so it converts)

- **Research them first** — bio, firm practice areas (antitrust, finance/securities,
  healthcare, energy, IP/labor). Bring 4–5 real questions.
- **Good questions:** What does a typical case look like? What separates associates
  who thrive? How did the PhD-to-consulting transition feel? What should I be doing
  now to be a strong applicant? How does your firm's recruiting timeline work?
- **Have a crisp 60-second JMP pitch ready.** Lead with the memorable hook —
  "this is how water systems game enforcement" (MR↔MCL strategic substitution) —
  not the 2SLS.
- **Close with two asks:** (1) "Is there anyone else you'd suggest I talk to?"
  (grows the funnel), (2) "When recruiting opens, would it be okay if I reached
  back out?" (sets up the referral).
- **Thank-you within 24 hours**, referencing something specific they said.

---

## Energy-sector angle — use it

The paper is about coal, mining, and environmental regulation. Firms with
energy / environmental / regulatory practices (Brattle and NERA strong here;
AG and CRA have energy work) will find the topic directly relevant. Lead with
that — you're not a generic candidate, you've done real energy-regulation
empirical work.

---

## Timing

Consulting full-time recruiting for fall-2026 PhD candidates typically opens
late summer / early fall, and good firms move fast.

- **June–July:** do informational interviews now, while people aren't swamped and
  you're a curiosity rather than one of 300 résumés.
- **August:** circle back — "Recruiting's about to open, I enjoyed our chat, would
  you be comfortable referring me?"
- **Fall:** applications + referrals land together.

Goal: by the time you apply, 3–4 people inside these firms already know your name
and your paper.

---

## Realistic volume

Aim for **15–25 outreach messages** over the summer. ~1/3 respond → 5–10 real
conversations. You need a handful of people who'll vouch for you, not a hundred.

---

## Mindset

PhD students resist this because it feels transactional. Reframe: you're a
competent researcher exploring a career, and people like talking about their path
and helping someone from their program. Worst case is silence. The cost of *not*
doing it is applying cold into a stack where referred candidates get the interviews.
